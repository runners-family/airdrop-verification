# Evidence projection and trust boundary

The public data is a new, schema-allowlisted projection of frozen historical captures. Its hashes identify the exported bytes. It is not presented as the untouched original response, and a hash of a private capture is not provider authentication.

The exporter verifies the privately frozen inputs against their existing manifests before transformation. A separate private original-to-export crosswalk supports local review. Public provenance contains neutral relative paths, transformation/profile labels and exported digests; original transport records and rich identity-content hashes are withheld. Public upstream SDK/layout hashes may be retained.

## Included facts

The artifact keeps the facts used by the deterministic rules: participant and necessary public chain addresses, program IDs, mint and position identities, required account bytes and permission fields, instruction beneficiary/amount evidence, lamport/token balances, chain slots, UTC capture times, pagination parameters/cursors, relevant protocol/index values, typed service categories, and the organizer roster. All amounts retain original integer values or exact decimal literals.

The replay independently rebuilds candidate universes, discovery/page chains, current holdings or historical qualifying actions, account/service screens, category lists, cluster decisions and the allocation. Captured API/index assertions remain external inputs where the criterion relies on that source.

## Omitted fields

The schemas omit transport locations and headers, RPC HTTP/WS endpoints, credentials, collector configurations and logs, local filesystem/computer information, social handles, personal identity names, images, unrelated URLs and operational receipts. Required public addresses are not labelled as belonging to an owner, operator or contributor.

Transaction projections retain the account keys, loaded addresses, balances, selected native/SPL instruction semantics and program-specific instruction prefixes needed by the reviewed attribution algorithms. Generic memo text, logs, arbitrary return data and unused custom instruction payloads are omitted. **A complete signed transaction message is not preserved or authenticated by this projection.** Signatures are identifiers linking the facts; this replay does not claim to validate original message signatures.

Solfate retains the exact authorities, extensions, credential name/symbol/official URI, supply and holder facts, while removing arbitrary additional metadata pairs. Non-credential holder/product profiles also blank unused Token-2022 metadata name/symbol/URI strings; authorities and valuation-relevant extensions remain. Seeker's official pointer/group and raw holder proofs remain intact. A historical RPC token-owner NUL sentinel is retained as a known non-address placeholder, without allowing arbitrary non-address owner strings.

HTTP responses are projected through source-specific field schemas, including nested objects. Service identity responses keep only address/type/category facts used by the service exemption rule. The old shared service map is regenerated from its pinned public exchange rows and issuer/market source captures, rather than trusted as an unexplained address registry.

## Checks and limits

The privacy scanner opens gzip files, nested/embedded JSON and relevant base64/base58 data. It reports aggregate findings only, and can use a separately supplied private marker list. Exact public documentation/license/credential URLs have a reviewed allowlist; RPC endpoint URLs are not allowed. Archive paths, links and extraction sizes are checked, and the complete extracted file inventory is hash-verified.

Privacy scanning and allowlists cannot prove that a public address has no outside association. The package deliberately retains decision-bearing public chain facts while withholding private role associations. No current balance is substituted for missing historical evidence.

Unsigned RPC/API captures, captured protocol values and the IslandDAO organizer roster remain source assertions. A deterministic replay authenticates neither their original provenance nor global provider completeness. Official protocol layouts, matching historical SDK computations, alternate-source checks and independently established chain commitments can strengthen particular claims; they are not blanket authenticity guarantees.
